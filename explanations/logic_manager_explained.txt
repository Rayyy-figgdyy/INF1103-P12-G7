LOGIC_MANAGER.PY EXPLAINED
A plain-English guide for teammates who don't write much code

This guide walks through logic_manager.py, the file that holds our business
rules. You don't need to know Python to follow it. Every number in the
examples below was checked by running the real functions from the file.


WHAT THIS FILE IS FOR

When a car owner checks a part (for example Engine Oil), the AI reads the
car's manual and gives an opinion: an urgency score from 1 to 10 and how
confident it is (High, Medium or Low). That opinion on its own is not the
final answer. This file takes the AI's opinion, adds the hard facts (how far
the car has been driven, how many days have passed), and applies OUR rules
to decide what the owner should do.

It answers three questions for every check:

1. What are the facts? Is the part overdue, how much does the owner drive,
   when does the COE end, and by what date should the part be serviced?
   (the function evaluate)
2. How serious is it, as a number from 0 to 100? This is only used to put
   the most serious items at the top of the urgent list. (the function score)
3. What is the final advice? For example "URGENT - Service immediately" or
   "OK - Not urgent". (the function route)

There is also a small fourth job: deciding whether a saved check belongs in
the "urgent / overdue items" list (the function is_urgent).

Why is the maths done here and not by the AI? Working out "69,500 minus
58,000" or "how many days between two dates" is simple arithmetic. An AI can
get numbers wrong, so we never rely on it for them. The AI gives its
judgement; this file does the counting.

This file never shows anything on screen, never talks to the AI and never
reads or writes files. It only takes information in and hands answers back.
That makes it easy to test on its own.


WHERE IT FITS

The app is split into four "managers", and main.py passes each maintenance
check through them in this order:

    io_manager  ->  ai_manager  ->  logic_manager  ->  data_manager
    (screen)        (asks AI)       (THIS FILE)        (saves to files)

A "record" is one maintenance check: one part, on one car, on one day. By the
time the record reaches this file it already contains:

1. What the user typed: last service date, odometer at that service, and
   the current odometer reading.
2. The car's details: model and COE expiry date.
3. The service interval for that part, from our stored schedule
   (cars.json), for example 10,000 km or 12 months.
4. The AI's answer: urgency_score, confidence, and ai_status ("ok" if the
   AI gave a valid answer, "unavailable" if no AI could answer).

main.py then uses this file in three steps:

    final_record.update(logic_manager.evaluate(enriched))
    final_record["risk_score"] = logic_manager.score(final_record)
    final_record["outcome"] = logic_manager.route(final_record)

In words: evaluate works out the facts and they are added to the record;
score adds a risk_score; route adds the final outcome. The finished record
then goes to data_manager to be saved and to io_manager to be shown.

Later, when the user picks menu option 3 (urgent items), main.py uses
is_urgent to keep only the checks that need attention, and sorts them by
risk_score, biggest first.


THE SETTINGS AT THE TOP

At the top of the file are four fixed settings. Their names are in CAPITALS,
which in Python is a signal that "this value never changes while the app is
running". Keeping them in one place means a rule can be adjusted by editing
a single line.

    SAFETY_CRITICAL_PARTS = ("brake pads", "tyres")

The parts where a failure is a safety risk. Worn brake pads mean the car may
not stop in time; worn tyres lose grip on the road. These two parts get extra
urgency in the rules. They are written in small letters on purpose: the code
turns the part name into small letters before comparing, so "Brake Pads" and
"brake pads" both match. The other four parts (Engine Oil, Spark Plugs,
Engine Air Filter, Coolant) are not safety-critical.

    DAYS_PER_MONTH = 30.44

Service intervals are given in months, but we count days. Months are not all
the same length, so we use the average month length, 30.44 days (365.25 days
in a year divided by 12, rounded). So 12 months becomes 365 days and 24
months becomes 731 days (after rounding to a whole number).

    COE_DEFER_WINDOW_DAYS = 180

How close the COE (Certificate of Entitlement) expiry has to be before we
say "the COE ends soon". 180 days is about 6 months. Used by the COE defer
rule in route.

    URGENT_PREFIXES = ("URGENT", "FLAGGED", "DUE SOON", "COE EXPIRED",
                       "NEEDS MANUAL REVIEW")

The starting words of every outcome that needs the owner's attention. Any
saved check whose outcome begins with one of these appears in menu option 3.
"NEEDS MANUAL REVIEW" is included because if the AI could not assess the
part, a person should look at it. "DEFER", "MONITOR" and "OK" are not in the
list, because those outcomes do not need action now.


WALKTHROUGH, FUNCTION BY FUNCTION

The functions appear below in the same order as in the file. A "function" is
a named set of steps: you give it some information, it does its job, and it
gives back ("returns") an answer.


1. to_date(value)

What it does: turns a date saved as text, like "2026-03-20", into a real
date that Python can do maths with (for example, subtract one date from
another).

What it needs: the saved text. It is allowed to be missing or wrong.

What it gives back: the date, or None (Python's word for "nothing") if the
text is missing or isn't a valid date. It never crashes on bad input.

Example (checked):

    to_date("2026-03-20")  gives  20 March 2026
    to_date("hello")       gives  None
    to_date(None)          gives  None


2. add_days(start, days)

What it does: works out the date that is a number of days after a starting
date. Python takes care of month ends and leap years.

What it needs: a starting date and a number of days.

What it gives back: the new date.

Example (checked):

    add_days(12 Oct 2026, 3)   gives  15 Oct 2026
    add_days(12 Oct 2026, 14)  gives  26 Oct 2026


3. recommend_service_date(check_date, is_overdue, km_left, days_left,
                          km_per_day, urgency, safety_critical)

This is BUSINESS RULE 3: it picks the "service by" date. It is called by
evaluate, which hands over all the facts it has already worked out.

What it needs:

1. check_date: the day of the check ("today").
2. is_overdue: True if the part has already passed its limit.
3. km_left: km left before the km limit (None if the part has no km limit).
4. days_left: days left before the time limit (None if no time limit).
5. km_per_day: how many km the owner drives per day, on average.
6. urgency: the AI's urgency score, 1 to 10 (None if the AI gave none).
7. safety_critical: True for brake pads and tyres.

What it gives back: the recommended "service by" date.

How it decides, in two parts:

Part A, the schedule's due date.
If the part is already overdue, it is due today. Otherwise the code looks at
both limits and takes whichever runs out FIRST:

1. The time limit: today plus the days left.
2. The km limit: km left divided by km per day gives the number of days
   until the km limit is reached at this owner's own driving pace. The
   decimals are dropped (51.5 becomes 51), which keeps us on the safe,
   earlier side.

If neither limit can be worked out (for example the part has no time
limit stored and the owner's driving pace can't be measured yet), it plays
safe and says today.

Part B, the AI's urgency can bring the date forward.

1. Urgency 8, 9 or 10: service within 3 days.
2. Urgency 5, 6 or 7: service within 14 days.
3. Urgency 1 to 4, or no score: keep the date from Part A.

For brake pads and tyres, those windows are halved and rounded down: 3 days
becomes 1 day, and 14 days becomes 7 days (never less than 1 day). The final
answer is whichever is EARLIER: the Part A date or the urgency deadline. So
the AI can only bring the date forward, never push it later.

Worked example (checked). Engine Oil, every 10,000 km or 12 months (365
days). Last serviced at 58,000 km on 20 March 2026. Checked on 12 October
2026 at 66,000 km, so NOT yet overdue.

1. 8,000 km driven in 206 days, so the pace is 8,000 / 206 = about 38.83 km
   a day. 2,000 km are left, and 365 - 206 = 159 days are left.
2. Part A, time limit: 12 Oct 2026 + 159 days = 20 Mar 2027.
3. Part A, km limit: 2,000 / 38.83 = 51.5, cut to 51 days, so
   12 Oct 2026 + 51 days = 2 Dec 2026.
4. The earlier of the two is 2 Dec 2026.
5. Part B with AI urgency 6: within 14 days = 26 Oct 2026. That is earlier
   than 2 Dec 2026, so the answer is 26 Oct 2026.
6. With AI urgency 3 instead, Part B does nothing and the answer stays
   2 Dec 2026.
7. If the same numbers were for brake pads with urgency 6, the window would
   be 7 days instead of 14, giving 19 Oct 2026.


4. evaluate(record)

What it does: all the arithmetic for one check. It works out:

1. km driven since the last service, and days passed since it.
2. Whether the part is overdue, and by how many km and days.
3. How many km the owner drives per day.
4. Whether the part is safety-critical (brake pads or tyres).
5. How many days until the COE expires (a negative number means it has
   already expired).
6. The recommended "service by" date (by calling
   recommend_service_date above).

What it needs: one record (one maintenance check).

What it gives back: a NEW collection of results with these labels:

    km_since_service, days_since_service, km_overdue, days_overdue,
    is_overdue, is_safety_critical, km_per_day, coe_days_left,
    recommended_service_date

The record it was given is not changed; main.py adds these results to the
record afterwards.

THE OVERDUE RULE. A part is overdue when EITHER limit is reached:

    km since the service   is at or over the km limit,  OR
    days since the service is at or over the month limit (as days)

This matches how car manuals word it: "every 10,000 km or 12 months,
whichever comes first". If a part has no km limit (or no time limit) stored,
that limit is simply skipped.

A few safety nets are built in:

1. No check date saved: today's date is used.
2. No last-service date saved: the check date is used, which simply means
   "0 days since the service".
3. A missing number is treated as 0, so the maths still works.
4. km and days since the service can never go below 0, even if the data has
   a typing mistake.
5. If 0 days have passed, km per day is set to 0 (you can't divide by 0).
6. km_per_day is rounded to 1 decimal place before it is saved.

Worked example (checked). Engine Oil, every 10,000 km or 12 months. Last
serviced at 58,000 km on 20 March 2026. Checked on 12 October 2026 at
69,500 km.

1. km since service = 69,500 - 58,000 = 11,500 km.
2. days since service = 20 Mar 2026 to 12 Oct 2026 = 206 days.
3. km limit: 11,500 is at least 10,000, so overdue by km, by 1,500 km.
4. time limit: 12 x 30.44 = 365.28, rounded to 365 days. 206 is under 365,
   so not overdue by time (days_overdue = 0, 159 days left).
5. One limit is enough, so is_overdue = True.
6. Pace = 11,500 / 206 = 55.83, saved as 55.8 km a day.
7. Overdue, so the recommended service date is today: 2026-10-12.

A second example where the TIME limit is the one that is broken (checked).
Brake Pads, every 40,000 km or 24 months (731 days). Last serviced at
30,000 km on 1 September 2024. Checked on 12 October 2026 at 60,000 km.

1. km since service = 30,000 km, which is under 40,000 (not over by km).
2. days since service = 771 days, which is over 731, so overdue by 40 days.
3. is_overdue = True, and is_safety_critical = True.


5. score(record)

What it does: gives the check a risk score from 0 to 100. The score is only
used to SORT the urgent list (most serious first). It never changes the
advice the owner gets.

What it needs: one record, including the AI's urgency score.

What it gives back: a whole number from 0 to 100.

How the points add up:

1. Start with AI urgency x 10 (so urgency 7 = 70 points).
2. Add 15 if the part is overdue.
3. Add 10 if it is a safety-critical part (brake pads or tyres).
4. If the total is over 100, it is cut down to 100.
5. If the AI gave no urgency score at all, the score is simply 0.

Worked examples (checked):

1. Engine Oil, overdue by 1,500 km, AI urgency 7:
   70 + 15 = 85. Not a safety part. Score = 85.
2. Brake Pads, overdue by 40 days, AI urgency 4:
   40 + 15 + 10 = 65. Score = 65.
3. Tyres, overdue, AI urgency 9:
   90 + 15 + 10 = 115, which is over 100, so the score is 100.
4. AI unavailable (no urgency score): score = 0.


6. route(record)

What it does: makes the final decision for one check. It goes through a list
of rules from top to bottom, and the FIRST rule that matches gives the
answer. The full list is in the next section.

What it needs: one record, including the AI's urgency score, confidence and
ai_status.

What it gives back: the outcome as text, for example
"URGENT - Service immediately".

Worked examples (checked):

1. Engine Oil overdue by 1,500 km, AI urgency 7, confidence High, COE far
   away: not urgency 8 or more, not a safety part, COE not ending soon, but
   it IS overdue, so "DUE SOON - Overdue by manufacturer schedule".
2. Brake Pads overdue by 40 days, AI urgency 4: rule 5 applies, so
   "URGENT - Safety-critical part overdue", even though the AI said 4.
3. Engine Oil not overdue (4,000 km and 206 days since service), COE ends
   on 31 Jan 2027 (111 days away), AI urgency 3:
   "DEFER - COE expires soon, non-critical service can wait".
4. The same Engine Oil check but with the COE years away: "OK - Not urgent".
   With urgency 5 instead of 3: "MONITOR - Service approaching".
5. COE ended on 30 Sep 2026, checked 12 Oct 2026 (coe_days_left = -12):
   "COE EXPIRED - Renew the COE or deregister before servicing".
6. AI could not answer: "NEEDS MANUAL REVIEW - AI assessment unavailable".


7. is_urgent(record)

What it does: looks at a saved check's outcome and says whether it needs the
owner's attention, by checking whether the outcome starts with one of the
words in URGENT_PREFIXES.

What it needs: one saved record (with its "outcome").

What it gives back: True (needs attention) or False.

Examples (checked):

    "DUE SOON - Overdue by manufacturer schedule"  gives  True
    "OK - Not urgent"                              gives  False
    a record with no outcome at all                gives  False

main.py uses this to build the list for menu option 3.


THE DECISION ORDER IN ROUTE()

route checks these rules in this exact order. As soon as one matches, it
stops and gives that outcome; the rules below it are never looked at.

1. The AI could not answer (ai_status is not "ok", or there is no urgency
   score):
       NEEDS MANUAL REVIEW - AI assessment unavailable
2. The COE has already expired (coe_days_left is below 0):
       COE EXPIRED - Renew the COE or deregister before servicing
3. AI urgency is 8 or more AND the AI's confidence is "High":
       URGENT - Service immediately
4. AI urgency is 8 or more (so confidence is Medium, Low or anything other
   than High):
       FLAGGED - High urgency but low AI confidence, verify with a mechanic
5. The part is brake pads or tyres AND it is overdue:
       URGENT - Safety-critical part overdue
6. The COE ends within 180 days AND the part is not brake pads or tyres AND
   AI urgency is 4 or less:
       DEFER - COE expires soon, non-critical service can wait
7. The part is overdue:
       DUE SOON - Overdue by manufacturer schedule
8. AI urgency is 1, 2 or 3:
       OK - Not urgent
9. Anything else (not overdue, urgency 4 to 7):
       MONITOR - Service approaching

Rules 3 and 4 together are our MULTI-CONDITION RULE, which the project brief
requires: they combine TWO things the AI told us, the urgency AND the
confidence. The same high urgency leads to a different outcome depending on
how sure the AI is (in practice, how clearly the manual covers the part).
Rule 6 is a second multi-condition rule (COE date, part type and urgency).

Why the order matters, with examples:

1. Rule 1 comes first because every rule after it uses the AI's urgency. If
   there is no urgency score, those rules would make no sense.
2. Rule 2 comes before any servicing advice because a car with an expired
   COE can't legally be driven in Singapore, so "service immediately" would
   be pointless advice.
3. Rules 3 and 4 come before rule 5 so that a very high urgency is never
   watered down. Overdue brake pads with urgency 9 and High confidence get
   "URGENT - Service immediately"; with Medium confidence they get FLAGGED
   for a mechanic to confirm.
4. Rule 5 comes before rule 6 so that brake pads or tyres can never be
   deferred. (Rule 6 also checks "not a safety part" itself, as a second
   guard.)
5. Rule 5 also comes before rule 7. Without that, overdue brake pads with a
   low AI score would only get "DUE SOON" instead of "URGENT".
6. Rule 6 comes before rule 7 on purpose: an overdue oil change on a car
   whose COE ends in a few months, with low AI urgency, is deferred rather
   than marked DUE SOON, because the car may soon be off the road.
7. Rules 8 and 9 are only reached by parts that are not overdue and have
   urgency under 8, so they simply split "fine" from "keep an eye on it".


FULL WORKED EXAMPLE

The situation:

    Car:               Mazda 3 Hatchback 2020
    COE expiry:        15 June 2030 (2030-06-15)
    Part:              Engine Oil
    Interval:          every 10,000 km or 12 months, whichever comes first
    Last serviced:     20 March 2026 at 58,000 km
    Checked:           12 October 2026 at 69,500 km
    AI's answer:       urgency 8, confidence Medium, ai_status "ok"

Every figure below was produced by running the real functions.

STEP 1, evaluate: the facts.

1. km since service: 69,500 - 58,000 = 11,500 km.
2. Days since service: from 20 Mar 2026 to 12 Oct 2026 is 206 days.
3. Interval in days: 12 x 30.44 = 365.28, rounded to 365 days.
4. Overdue by km? 11,500 is at least 10,000, so yes, by 1,500 km.
5. Overdue by time? 206 is less than 365, so no (days_overdue = 0, with
   159 days to spare).
6. Either limit is enough, so is_overdue = True.
7. km per day: 11,500 / 206 = 55.83, saved as 55.8.
8. Safety-critical? "engine oil" is not brake pads or tyres, so False.
9. COE days left: 12 Oct 2026 to 15 Jun 2030 = 1,342 days. Positive, so the
   COE is still valid and is not "ending soon" (more than 180 days).

STEP 2, the "service by" date (recommend_service_date, inside evaluate).

1. Part A: the part is already overdue, so it is due today, 12 Oct 2026.
2. Part B: urgency 8 means "within 3 days" (not halved, because engine oil
   is not safety-critical), which would be 15 Oct 2026.
3. The earlier of 12 Oct and 15 Oct is 12 Oct.
4. recommended_service_date = 2026-10-12.

What evaluate hands back:

    km_since_service          11500
    days_since_service        206
    km_overdue                1500
    days_overdue              0
    is_overdue                True
    is_safety_critical        False
    km_per_day                55.8
    coe_days_left             1342
    recommended_service_date  2026-10-12

STEP 3, score: the risk score.

1. Urgency 8 x 10 = 80.
2. Overdue, so + 15 = 95.
3. Not safety-critical, so nothing more.
4. 95 is not over 100, so risk_score = 95.

STEP 4, route: the decision.

1. Rule 1: ai_status is "ok" and there is an urgency score, so move on.
2. Rule 2: coe_days_left is 1,342, not below 0, so move on.
3. Rule 3: urgency 8 is 8 or more, but confidence is Medium, not High, so
   this rule does not match.
4. Rule 4: urgency is 8 or more, so this rule matches. Stop here.

    outcome = "FLAGGED - High urgency but low AI confidence, verify with a
               mechanic"

(If the AI had said confidence High, the outcome would have been
"URGENT - Service immediately" instead. This is the multi-condition rule in
action.)

STEP 5, is_urgent: does it go on the urgent list?

The outcome starts with "FLAGGED", which is in URGENT_PREFIXES, so
is_urgent gives True. This check will appear in menu option 3, sorted by its
risk score of 95.


PYTHON IDEAS USED IN THIS FILE

1. import. "import datetime" borrows Python's built-in toolbox for dates,
   like taking a calculator out of a drawer instead of building one.

2. Constants in CAPITALS. Names like DAYS_PER_MONTH are settings that never
   change, like the rules printed on a board game's box lid.

3. Tuple, written with round brackets ( ). A fixed list that can't be
   changed once made, like a printed menu rather than a whiteboard.

4. Function (def ... return). A named recipe: you hand it ingredients, it
   follows the steps, and "return" hands the finished dish back. A return
   also ends the recipe at once, which is how route stops at the first rule
   that matches.

5. Dictionary and .get(). A record is a dictionary: a form with labelled
   boxes. record.get("check_date") reads one box, and gives None instead of
   an error if the box is missing, like being told "that line is blank".

6. None. Python's word for "nothing / no answer", for example when there is
   no COE date or the AI gave no urgency score.

7. "x or y" as a fallback. "to_date(...) or datetime.date.today()" means
   "use the saved date, but if there isn't one, use today", like "pay by
   card, or cash if the card doesn't work".

8. if / elif / else. A fork in the road: check the first condition, and
   only if it fails try the next ("elif" = "else if"), and if none match
   take the "else" path.

9. and / or / not. "and" needs both sides true (overdue AND a brake part);
   "or" needs at least one (over the km limit OR over the time limit);
   "not" flips true and false.

10. try / except. "Try this, and if it goes wrong, do that instead of
    crashing", like trying a key in a lock and calmly moving on if it
    doesn't fit. Used by to_date for bad date text.

11. min() and max(). min picks the smallest value, max the biggest. For
    dates, the smallest is the earliest, which is how we pick "whichever
    comes first". max(0, ...) is a floor that stops a value going below 0;
    min(100, ...) is a ceiling that caps the risk score.

12. int(), round() and //. int() drops the decimals (51.5 becomes 51),
    round() rounds to the nearest whole number (365.28 becomes 365), and //
    divides then rounds down (3 // 2 = 1, 14 // 2 = 7).

13. Dates and timedelta. Subtracting two dates gives a length of time, and
    .days reads it as a number of days. Adding a timedelta to a date jumps
    forward on the calendar, like counting squares on a wall calendar.

14. Tidying text: .strip().lower(). Removes spaces at the ends and turns
    everything into small letters, so "Brake Pads " becomes "brake pads".

15. in. Checks whether something is in a collection, like checking whether
    a name is on a guest list.

16. .startswith(). Checks how a piece of text begins, like reading only the
    first word on a label. Given a tuple of words, it is true if the text
    starts with ANY of them.

17. Type hints such as "-> bool" or "-> datetime.date". Notes for the
    reader saying what kind of value goes in and comes out.
    Python does not enforce them; they are like labels on storage boxes.


PROJECT RULES THIS FILE FOLLOWS

1. Brief section 2.3, the Logic layer: the brief asks for evaluate(record),
   score(record) and route(record). All three are in this file with exactly
   those names.

2. Brief, multi-condition rule: at least one rule must combine two of the
   AI's outputs. Rules 3 and 4 in route combine the urgency score AND the
   confidence. Rule 6 (COE defer) is a further multi-condition rule.

3. Business rule 2, manual guidebook: service intervals come from our
   stored schedule (cars.json), loaded by data_manager and placed in the
   record by main.py. This file only reads interval_km and interval_months
   from the record; it never makes up an interval.

4. Business rule 3, service date: recommend_service_date sets a
   "service by" date using how important the part is (safety-critical parts
   get half the time), how much the owner drives (km per day), the odometer
   (km left) and the AI's urgency score.

5. Singapore COE rules: an expired COE gives "COE EXPIRED", and a COE
   ending within 180 days lets low-urgency, non-safety servicing be
   deferred.

6. Safety first: overdue brake pads or tyres always get an URGENT outcome,
   whatever the AI said, and they can never be deferred.

7. Every check goes through the AI (hard constraint C2), but the app must
   not break when the AI is unavailable. When ai_manager hands over its
   fallback answer, route gives "NEEDS MANUAL REVIEW" and score gives 0, so
   a person is asked to look at it.

8. Numbers are never left to the AI: all km, day and date calculations are
   done here with plain arithmetic.

9. Separation of layers: this file has no screen, AI or file code. Business
   rule 1 (the odometer can't go backwards) is checked while the user types,
   in io_manager.py, not here.
